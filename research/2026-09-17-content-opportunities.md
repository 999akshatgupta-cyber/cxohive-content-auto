## 1. Same-day exit letter. Or handover to another hub until 15 December. “We have several open roles.”

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10

One group got the letter on 15 September. The other was kept until 15 December to hand the work to colleagues at other technology hubs. The work stays. The name on it may not.

- Town hall on 15 Sep 2026. Adidas confirmed a reduction of roles in its Technology organisation in India.  
- Company statement: impact is **less than 20%** of the India Technology organisation. Early media (Wocult / HT / NDTV) had ~350 of ~700, nearly half. Do not write 50% as fact.

These changes are intended to simplify parts of our organisation, strengthen critical capabilities, and position adidas for long-term success in a rapidly evolving business and technology environment.”  
- “The impact of these changes is less than 20% of our India Technology organisation. We continue to invest in strategic capabilities and have several open roles across the organisation. - “India remains a strategically important market for Adidas, and our Technology Hub continues to play a critical role in enabling the company's growth, innovation, and digital transformation ambitions.” (India Today)

## 3. Mandate up. Headcount flat. The gap is mid-career, not campus.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named 16 Sep ETGCC survey + quotes; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News / Trend (ETGCC) + named operator quotes (country-head / HR-adjacent mix)

**Why the ICP cares**  
This is the 15–20 year seat. The centre is being asked to take on harder work and sit closer to outcomes without adding people. ETGCC’s internal survey put numbers on it. Progress Software’s India country manager then said the quiet part: entry-level adapts fastest; the real work is helping **mid-career** talent bridge the gap. That is the ICP, named.

**Supporting evidence**  
Bhuvana Kamath, ETGCC. Published 16 Sep 2026, 15:07 IST; updated 15:51 IST.  
https://gcc.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry-trends/the-leaner-gcc-on-decoupling-growth-from-headcount/134283923  

Facts the piece directly establishes:  
- ETGCCWorld internal survey, **300+** respondents across tech, BFSI, healthcare, engineering, retail/consumer.  
- **27.6%** said team size grew by up to 25%. **37%** said team size remained broadly unchanged. **18.2%** reported a decline.  
- 78% said their teams work with external vendors at least occasionally.  
- Nearly half of GCCs surveyed operate on hybrid infrastructure; 13.6% rely primarily on public cloud.  
- Only **32%** of respondents had completed a certification or training programme in the past year.  
- Tesco India is using “skill-density” as a metric (Somnath Baishya, people director — HR voice, use as colour, not as ICP).  
- Tesco 4B: build, buy, borrow, bot.

**Direct quotes:**  
- Nithya Cadambi, GM – global centres of excellence, Commvault: “Beyond core AI and automation talent, GCCs are prioritising cybersecurity, data management, product engineering, customer experience, and digital resilience. Now, the common thread is greater end-to-end ownership of global products and business critical functions.”  
- Cadambi: “Hiring alone cannot meet the pace at which business needs are changing.”  
- Somnath Baishya, Tesco: “At Tesco, we are investing on skill-density to propel expertise: colleagues who deeply understand the business, combine technology and data with commercial judgement, and take end-to-end ownership.”  
- Girija Kolagada, country manager – India & VP, HR business partnering, APAC, Progress Software (**HR / country-manager, not seed ICP**): “I've seen the nature of hard problems shift; the technical challenges are increasingly solvable, but building teams that can adapt, unlearn and re-skill at pace is the harder, more human problem.”  
- Kolagada: “Our own experience tells us the biggest gaps aren't always where you'd expect. Entry-level engineers often adapt fastest, while the real work is helping mid-career talent bridge that gap. Investing in that transition, not just in hiring, is what will determine India's next phase of engineering leadership.”  
- Rajiv Gajria, VP and MD, Blackbaud India (country-head adjacent): “The opportunity in India is therefore no longer defined by access to talent alone. The more important question is what organisations choose to build with that talent and how much ownership they are prepared to place here.”  
- Navaneet Mishra, head and SVP, Hexagon R&D India: “It’s important to go beyond simply executing tasks and truly understand the problems that we are solving, who we impact, and how sustainable the solution will be for the larger business in the long run. It’s about taking ownership of the problem and thinking about the impact our work will have over time.”  
- Teja Manakame, VP product and engineering, Curriculum Associates: “The gap isn't volume, it's specialisation at the intersection of domains. India produces enormous technical talent, but people who can sit between AI, product, and a specific domain are in high demand.”

**What appears to be working**  
Survey splits (37% flat / 18% down) plus one sentence that names mid-career. Not another “GCCs are strategic now.”

**Underlying idea**  
Headcount is no longer the proxy for whether the India seat grew. If the file still counts people managed and tickets closed, the centre can get more important while the person stays illegible.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
Positioning + evidence. Do not run Maisekar “accountability vs ownership” or Saraiya SOP this week. Do not lecture reskilling. The CXO Hive cut is: the org is measuring skill-density and end-to-end ownership; their promotion pack still measures delivery volume. Mid-career is the gap the country HR named — the ICP is that gap.

**Suggested direction**  
Hook: “37% of GCC teams did not grow. The mandate did. The person they are worried about is mid-career, not campus.” Then Kolagada’s two sentences. Close on Gajria: how much ownership did they actually place here — in the brief, not the all-hands.

## 4. Someone saw the seat before they felt ready. That is still how the next room works.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named 15 Sep ETGCC interview; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News / Creator-adjacent (ETGCC Faces of Change) — country-head calibration

**Why the ICP cares**  
The stall after Senior Manager is not a skills gap. It is that nobody one level up put them in a room they did not yet look ready for. Lalitha Indrakanti said that as a career fact, not a coaching slogan: someone trusted her before she could see it, and she still leads that way. The ICP already did the 12–25 years. They are waiting for that person.

**Supporting evidence**  
ETGCC Desk. Published 15 Sep 2026, 10:13 IST; updated 10:27 IST. SURGE 2026 photo-essay.  
https://gcc.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/people-culture/faces-of-change-lalitha-indrakanti/134251995  

Facts the piece directly establishes:  
- Lalitha Indrakanti, CEO, Jaguar Land Rover – Technology and Business Services India. **Country-head / C-suite. Calibration, not seed ICP.**  
- Early-career: given an opportunity bigger than she thought she was ready for.  
- Unlearned: a leader must always appear certain.  
- Raised by an aunt who began working in 1956 at 23, 34 years in education, became a headmistress, sent to schools that needed transformation.  
- After 10 years at IBM, left a comfortable senior role in **2014** to build Cargill’s GCC in India from scratch; Cargill did not then have structured business services elsewhere; they built ~3,000 people.  
- On local-global: enterprise-first mindset; “as more global roles are moving to India, distributed decision making is inevitable.”

**Direct quotes (Lalitha Indrakanti, ETGCC):**  
- “What stayed with me then, was not just the responsibility I was being given, but the trust that was showed on me. Someone had seen potential in me before I could fully see it myself.”  
- “I believe the most powerful thing a leader can give someone is an opportunity before they feel completely ready for it. People often grow into the opportunity they are given. I know I did.”  
- “Today, I have become much more comfortable saying, ‘This is what we know, this is what we do not know yet, and we will work it out together.’”  
- “The decision meant moving from consulting to the other side of the table. It was uncomfortable and that was precisely why I knew I had to consider it seriously.”  
- “That experience taught me that the decisions which change us most are often the ones we feel least ready to make.”  
- Wanted team sentence: “She challenged us to think bigger, gave us the confidence to question and never lost sight of the people behind the outcome.”

**What appears to be working**  
A sponsor-shaped memory, not a GCC-setup announcement. The ready/unready split is the ICP’s actual stall.

**Underlying idea**  
Promotion rooms do not wait for the person who feels ready. They wait for the person someone will argue for. Presence without that argument is still a delivery title.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
Sponsorship + presence. Do not clone coffee-check, SOP-given task, or “just exit.” Do not turn this into a tribute to JLR. The cut for Mudit’s audience: they have been ready enough to run the machine for years; nobody has given them the seat that is slightly too big. **Suggested direction**  
Hook: “Someone saw the seat before she felt ready. That is still the only way the next room works.” Then the IBM → Cargill 2014 line as proof she did it to herself once. End on: if nobody is carrying that sentence when they are not in the room, certainty in the standup will not substitute.
