## 1. India got operating control. The stake still says 49.9%.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named 13–14 Sep filings; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News (ET + FT) + LinkedIn conversation (calibration)

**Why the ICP cares**  
The India SM/Director already runs the work. The global room still treats them as delivery. PwC just put that split on paper: US keeps 50.1%, India keeps operating control of the US acceleration centres, and the US CEO said the sentence they sit in — this is not about where the work gets done.

**Supporting evidence**  
ET Bureau, Vinod Mahanta. Originally published 13 Sep 2026; last updated 14 Sep 2026, 00:05 IST. AMP body returned.  
https://m.economictimes.com/industry/services/consultancy-/-audit/pwc-india-us-to-create-40000-strong-consulting-jv-india-to-get-operating-control/articleshow/134183372.cms  

FT, same weekend: US and Indian units pool outsourcing and advisory in a 40,000-person JV, more than 10% of PwC’s global workforce. Deal signed Sunday.  
https://www.ft.com/content/bf1f8def-98ce-4976-acc3-cd80f0956ab0  

Facts ET directly establishes:  
- PwC India consulting + PwC US India acceleration centres → ~40,000 people at launch; ~$2bn day-one (from $1.3bn). Subject to CCI and other approvals.  
- PwC US 50.1% / PwC India 49.9%. Operating control, including of the US-run ACs, rests with PwC India. Jointly controlled.  
- JV houses management, technology and risk consulting plus the ACs. Audit, tax, deals, and some government advisory stay outside.  
- Sanjeev Krishan (chair, PwC India) will be CEO of the JV.  
- Aim: fivefold in three years vs Vision 2030’s threefold in five.  
- ET: the JV is meant to remove friction on mandates “won and led by partners in the US or Europe but increasingly delivered by India-based delivery centres.”  
- More than half of Big Four combined India revenue (~Rs 50,000 crore) now comes from technology and management consulting.

**Direct quotes (ET):**  
- Sanjeev Krishan: “This is PwC India being trusted to drive global growth.”  
- Paul Griggs, PwC US Senior Partner and CEO: “The firms that win will be the ones that stop thinking about markets, capabilities, and delivery as separate pieces.”  
- Griggs: “This isn't about where work gets done. It's about building something clients can't get anywhere else: the best of PwC, brought together around their biggest opportunities, wherever they operate.”  
- Krishan (ET paraphrase, attributed): the deal marks a shift “from being primarily an India market operation to becoming a larger engine for global consulting growth.”

**What appears to be working**  
A legal object, not another “India is strategic now” slide. 49.9% + operating control is a sentence an India Director can feel in their chest.

**Underlying idea**  
Control on the org chart is not the same as being the name the global room can explain. If the file still says “we delivered the AC work,” the JV did not move their seat.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
Positioning + evidence. Do not cheer PwC. Do not write a Big Four explainer. Ask whether their brief still lists delivery location, or the problem they now own because India runs the machine. Adjacent to Sunday’s “just exit” — do not reuse Panjwani’s sequence line.

**Suggested direction**  
Hook: “India got operating control. The stake still says 49.9%.” Then Griggs’s “isn't about where work gets done.” One question: which sentence is in their mid-year pack — location, or the mandate.

**Risks / saturation**  
Country-head / network-CEO voices, not ICP. LinkedIn conversation is Partners, GCC advisors, company pages. Easy to collapse into Sunday’s “just exit.” Keep the stake/control split; drop SURGE sequence language. Do not invent CCI outcomes or India pay.

## 4. The idea that went global started as a coffee with a junior.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named Hegde quotes; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News (ETGCC) + named operator interview

**Why the ICP cares**  
They are waiting for a formal innovation mandate. Sreedevi Hegde’s collections app did not start in a lab. It started when she sat with random people, a junior named a collections problem, and India built the thing customers now see. The stall is not “no ideas.” It is that nobody in the India seat is allowed to be responsible for one.

**Supporting evidence**  
Same 11 Sep ETGCC piece (URL above).

Facts the article directly establishes:  
- Sreedevi Hegde, SVP & country head, Acentra Health.  
- Practice: pick random people / coffee check. A junior raised a collections-process problem. Push incentives to the customer’s phone during collection, not later.  
- “We created the app here in India. We made that, and now it is coming up with the customers.”  
- No formal mandate.  
- Hegde on the old GCC model: centres “were designed that way” to execute, not to “think of creating the model itself.”  
- Fear of failure as the atmosphere. Her counter: sit beside them in their systems.

**Direct quotes (Hegde, ETGCC):**  
- “I have this practice of picking up random people from the organisations and teams, and then have the roundtable or just a coffee check.”  
- “Ideas will come from anybody. First thing is to identify that, try to nurture it and be responsible for that.”  
- “There is this atmosphere of our thinking of that failure. We always think okay, if we fail, what will happen?”  
- “I just go into their systems, sit beside them, and start working so that I understand.”  
- “Many of us coexist with the customers. That’s one of the things we need to do more–get closer to the business and the services, because that makes it easier for us to understand where we can really drive revenue or create value.”

## 5. If the customer is not paying, it is not a promotion file.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named Bishnoi quotes; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News (ETGCC) + named operator interview

**Why the ICP cares**  
India GCCs are full of POCs that never become a sentence a committee can buy. Navin Bishnoi’s test is colder than “be innovative”: if a customer is not paying, the innovation is of no use. His India team ported an enterprise security use case to the data centre, partnered a local university, and now sells it globally for “many million dollars.” The number is his claim in ETGCC, not a CXO Hive case study.

**Supporting evidence**  
Same 11 Sep ETGCC piece (URL above).

Facts the article directly establishes:  
- Navin Bishnoi, vice-president and India country head, Marvell India.  
- Problem: securing the data centre as AI compute grows.  
- Team in India built from “ground zero,” ported an enterprise use case to data centre, partnered a local university, now a product sold across the world.

**Direct quotes (Bishnoi, ETGCC):**  
- “What was coming in is something that we brought up completely from ground zero as the whole security part of it.”  
- “The team came from here and built it up, ported it from an enterprise use case into a data center use case, partnered with a local university to build the whole stack of the system.”  
- “Now that's a product that we sell across the world, generating many million dollars.”  
- “An innovative idea is to solve a customer problem. If a customer is not paying for it, then that innovation is of no use.”  
- “The innovation idea has to solve a problem, either for the customer as a feature or a product, or by getting it to market faster. That is what drives the transition from experimentation into something that goes into implementation.”

**What appears to be working**  
A yes/no commercial test. Usable in a mid-year pack.

**Underlying idea**  
A POC is activity. A paying customer is evidence. Most India Director files are still activity.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
Evidence. Do not write a Marvell product post. Do not invent the university name or the dollar figure beyond the quote. Ask which line in their pack a customer has already paid for.

**Suggested direction**  
Hook: “If the customer is not paying, it is not a promotion file.” One contrast: POC list vs paying-problem list.

**Risks / saturation**  
Country head. “Many million dollars” is his line — do not upgrade it. Adjacent to Tambe “touch the top line” (used Sunday). Keep the paying-customer test; drop “global ladder.” Own posts this week were CONNECT India / VDAT keynote congratulations, not this quote.
