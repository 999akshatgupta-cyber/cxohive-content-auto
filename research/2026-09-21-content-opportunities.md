## 1. The operating model is “reaching an expiry date.” AI should not automate accountability.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named 18 Sep ETGCC panel; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News / Trend (ETGCC SURGE panel)

**Why the ICP cares**  
The mid-senior in a GCC is still measured as if the 1990s bargain holds: cheaper execution, reported back. A Siemens India CEO just said that bargain’s operating model is hitting an expiry date. A Delta Capita country head then named the live risk: if the system absorbs the learning, the person overseeing it may never acquire it — and the regulator still wants a name.

**Supporting evidence**  
Bhuvana Kamath, ETGCC. Published 18 Sep 2026, 16:17 IST.  
https://gcc.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/people-culture/is-the-gcc-operating-model-already-past-its-expiry-date/134332868

Facts the piece directly establishes:
- Pankaj Vyas, CEO and MD, Siemens Technology and Services: four years ago GCC conferences talked about moving up the value chain, not “changing the fabric of the organisation.” Origin story was cost: “let's go to India to do software because I'm going to save cost.”
- Vyas: “Now comes AI, where it's really challenging the operating model that we have been working on. I personally believe this operating model is reaching an expiry date right here.” Continuing to optimise for ownership is insufficient; centres should build toward the top line rather than defend a cost position AI is likely to erode. He also said there will not be one template.
- Lalitha Indrakanti, CEO, JLR Technology and Business Services India (country-head; her 15 Sep Faces of Change was used 17 Sep — do not clone “opportunity before ready”): “The relationship balance now is no longer a question between headquarters and GCC. The relationship balance has to be between AI, headquarters and GCC. It's a three-way relationship.”
- Tilak Banerjee, head, Takeda ICC India: “AI is not really a magic wand. It's a magic mirror. It tells you where the gaps in your processes are. It tells you how bad your data is.” “If you do not have the human in the room, you do not understand the outcome of that intervention.”
- Pancham Taneja, country head, Delta Capita India: “It's not about the fact of how much work the centre can absorb. The question is now how much impact and the value the centre is creating.” “AI should not automate accountability. The regulator does not care how quickly the decision was made. All the regulator cares about is, behind the decision, who's the accountable person.”
- Bernd Otto Hörmann, CEO, MHP India: “Leadership has to flip.” More experienced team members have to be encouraged to use the technologies; newer entrants are frequently faster. “We are in the middle of it.”

**What appears to be working**  
A named expiry date plus a named person behind the decision. Not another “GCCs must move up the value chain.”

**Underlying idea**  
If AI is the mirror, the promotion file that still lists executed tasks is what it will show. Accountability that cannot be named is the seat that disappears.

## 2. The ladder is being redesigned. It will start on the third floor.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named 20 Sep FE opinion; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News / Opinion (Financial Express)

**Why the ICP cares**  
They were told AI would take the junior work and free them for judgement. FE’s Monday object is that the place judgement used to be manufactured — the bench, the fresher, the first outage — is being removed. The 15–20 year seat is the last cohort that learned by doing the work the machine now sits on. That is not a campus story. It is a promotion-file story: if the next layer never arrives, they remain the machine.

**Supporting evidence**  
Shyamal Majumdar, Financial Express. First uploaded 20 Sep 2026, 19:02 IST.  
https://www.financialexpress.com/opinion/wanted-fresher-3-years-experience-essential/4343446/

Facts the piece directly establishes:
- Job descriptions labelled “fresher” have begun demanding two or three years of practical project experience. Named as “experience creep.”
- “Hiring intent among IT firms has slipped, offer letters arrive with onboarding dates that recede like a mirage.”
- “Across the five largest firms, the combined workforce shrank last year even as every one of them grew. This is celebrated in earnings calls as productivity. On campus, it is experienced as silence.” The five firms are not named in the piece. Do not invent them.
- The old model: recruit in bulk, train for months, park surplus on the bench. “The bench was much mocked, but it was a national institution — a paid waiting room for the middle class.”
- “Now AI sits on the bench.” “Production-ready” asks the 21-year-old to complete, before her first day, the apprenticeship that work used to provide.
- Direct: “Senior engineers have been reassured that AI will handle the junior work, freeing them for judgement, architecture, and taste. But judgement is precisely what juniors used to acquire by doing junior work. If the machine performs every task on which experience is built, tomorrow’s seniors must arrive fully formed… or not at all. The industry has designed a workforce that demands experience while abolishing the place where experience was manufactured.”
- Planning horizon is the quarter; the missing cohort is “a problem for a decade hence.” The task force will recommend reskilling.
- Close: “The ladder is being redesigned. When it returns, it will be strategic, AI-enabled, and human-centric — and it will start on the third floor.”

**What appears to be working**  
A specific image (third floor) plus the senior-side paradox. Not a fresher-hiring rant.

**Underlying idea**  
The person who still has judgement because they did the junior work is now infrastructure for a system that will not replace them with a pipeline. Delivery got them here. The missing bench is not their problem until the committee treats them as the remaining cost line.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
Positioning + evidence. Do not write a campus-placement explainer. Do not recap FDE-army or “become an FDE.” Ask whether their mid-year pack still reads like the work AI now sits on, or like the judgement the piece says has nowhere to be learned. Adjacent commercial: the consult as a filter for people who can name the problem they own, not the tasks they used to train juniors on.

**Suggested direction**  
Hook: “The ladder is being redesigned. It will start on the third floor.” Then the senior paradox in two sentences. One question: if they are the last layer that learned by doing the junior work, what in the file makes them the person who owns what comes next — not the remaining bench.

**Risks / saturation**  
Easy to become a gen-Z jobs post. LinkedIn index on the phrases returned US coaches and a generic “fresher hiring is losing ground” post, not India SM–VP. The five firms are unnamed — do not fill them in. Do not invent the shrink percentage.
