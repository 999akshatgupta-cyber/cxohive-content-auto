## 1. Global titles landed in India. Decision rights did not.

**Final score:** 9.0 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH  
**Source type:** LinkedIn + News / ICP signal

**Why the ICP cares**  
GCC and India-site leaders are being given “global” and “product” language. The approval chain still sits at HQ. That is the GCC version of senior work without the rooms.

**Supporting evidence**  
Nasscom–Zinnov 2026 figures are being repeated by operators: 2,117 GCCs, 2.36 million people, $98.4bn, 96% of post-FY21 centres launched with a product/portfolio mandate, 64% of site leaders hold dual mandates. Separate posts keep making the same cut: mandate ≠ sign-off.

**What appears to be working**  
A specific test, not a GCC-boom cheer. “Who has to say yes if India kills a feature?” “Can they decide without sending it back to HQ?” First-person operating detail beats report recaps.

**Underlying idea**  
The title moved. The approval matrix did not. Accountability arrived before authority.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
This is sponsorship and positioning with a current market object. The person who already owns the work still cannot be explained, in one sentence, as the decision-maker. Do not turn it into “book a consult.”

**Suggested direction**  
Question or scene, not a list. Hook: “Your India title is global. Your approval chain is not.” Ask who signs off if they stop a feature, hire a director, or kill a roadmap item. Stay with employed operators, not GCC-setup vendors.

**Risks / saturation**  
GCC consultants and recruiters are flooding the same Nasscom–Zinnov numbers. Sarabjit Singh’s 95/28 post is an engagement ring (creators/marketers, comments clustered inside ~15 minutes). Sunil Khatwani (GCC advisor) appears on more than one post in this cluster — treat as adjacent, not ICP proof. Hindu BL “fractional CXO / ₹1Cr+” was already used this week; do not clone it.
