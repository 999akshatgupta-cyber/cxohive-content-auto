### Managers trusted the AI-assisted task less. The press cycle is 4 Nov.

Final score: 7.3. The paper is real. This week’s LinkedIn trace is one non-ICP post with 2 reactions. Evidence of attention for a slot this week is below 7, so the ceiling is 7.4. Evidence confidence: HIGH on the paper as Phys.org reports it, LOW on ICP reaction.

Phys.org, 4 Nov 2026, Hebrew University. Samuel Meyers, Yochanan Bigman, Roman Briker. The Leadership Quarterly, DOI 10.1016/j.leaqua.2026.102004. Six studies. In one experiment, supervisors chose whether to trust an employee’s report of time spent. They were not judging the work. People who did the task alone were trusted 68% of the time. People who used AI were trusted 24% of the time. Lower trust was associated with worse performance evaluations and lower perceived suitability for promotion. When the organization explicitly encouraged AI use, the trust penalty was substantially reduced and no longer statistically significant. A ban, or no policy, left the penalty in place. The supervisor’s own AI experience did not reliably remove it. Phys.org also describes a study of academic supervisors and graduate students, not India GCC managers.
