## 4. AI replaced the skill. It did not take the responsibility.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named 11 Sep quotes; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News (ET AI / GFF)

**Why the ICP cares**  
Their teams now ship more with assistants. The fear in the corridor is replacement. Policybazaar’s CTO said the opposite, and then the line that actually matters for a promotion file: skills can move; responsibility does not.

**Supporting evidence**  
ET Online, Arun Padmanabhan, last updated 11 Sep 2026 12:31 IST. Interview with Bibhu Krishna, CTO, Policybazaar, at GFF 2026. AMP scrape returned the body.  
https://m.economictimes.com/ai/ai-insights/gff-2026-ai-has-increased-work-not-replaced-jobs-at-policybazaar-says-cto-bibhu-krishna/amp_articleshow/134048024.cms  

Facts the article directly establishes:  
- “About 90% of the code that we generate today is written using AI” (Krishna).  
- Production releases: ~1,500/month a year and a half ago → 3,000–3,500 now.  
- “We have not asked any developer to go. We have not done that. In fact, we have hired.”  
- Pending tasks that lacked bandwidth are now being done; non-tech staff propose more automations.  
- Two agents in tandem (generate / check); 60–70% of issues resolved before production; a tech lead still reviews the rest.  
- “It’s very easy to generate a code using vibe coding. The difficult part is how to upkeep and do housekeeping of that.”  
- Motor insurance: AI decides in about half of video-inspection cases whether footage is enough.  
- “We need to have a human in the loop.”

**Direct quotes (Krishna, ET):**  
- “I think the job has increased, the work has increased rather than replacing or changing.”  
- “AI can just replace the skills, not the responsibilities.”  
- “The skills can be any, but responsibility is ours.”

Same-week adjacent (do not merge into this post): Axis Max Life COO Manu Lavanya, ET, 8 Sep 2026 — human in / on / out of the loop; “my head of operations now is also my chief digital officer”; “Business will become AI.”

**What appears to be working**

**Underlying idea**  
Output can be generated. The name on the incident, the customer answer, and the promotion brief is still a person. If the file only lists tools used, it is a skills list.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**

## 5. They want 8,900 FDEs. The market has about 1,500.

**Final score:** 8.4 / 10  
**Evidence confidence:** HIGH on the named ET/Quess numbers via the Apexon reprint; LOW on qualified ICP LinkedIn comments  
**Source type:** News (ET, 8 Sep) + named reprint

**Why the ICP cares**  
This is not another “Accenture reserved 1,000 seats” recap. The industry-wide ask is now TCS 8,900 this year, Infosys up to 6,000, Wipro 10,000 in 18 months, HCLTech 15–20% of fresher hiring — against Quess’s 1,300–1,500 “narrowly defined” FDEs on the market. The mid-layer is the conversion pool. That is the India SM/Director.

**Supporting evidence**  
ET, Shristi Achar. Mobile scrape was paywalled (synopsis only). Last-Modified in metadata: Tuesday 8 Sep 2026, 10:03 PM.  
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/a-new-army-of-forward-deployed-engineers-are-rewriting-the-playbook-at-indian-it-services-companies/articleshow/133896023.cms  

Apexon PDF reprint of the same ET piece (full quotes used below). Vendor reprint, not a second primary.  
https://www.apexon.com/pdf/Apexon_Srinikeths_quotes_in_The_Economic_Times.pdf  

Facts both establish:  
- TCS 8,900 FDEs this year; Infosys up to 6,000; HCLTech 15–20% of fresher hiring as FDEs; Wipro 10,000 FDEs in the next 18 months.  
- Quess: ~1,300–1,500 narrowly defined FDEs available now.  
- Adjacent pool 12,000–15,000 engineers with 55–65% skill overlap (Kapil Joshi, Quess IT staffing CEO).  
- Indeed: FDE job postings in India up ~19-fold / 1,807% YoY, June 2025–June 2026.  
- Services FDE ≠ Palantir/OpenAI FDE: collapse BA / architect / developer / tester / infra into one customer-facing person.  
- UST’s Manu Gopinath: customers want a firm that owns an outcome and an SLA, not just people on a project.  
- Apexon’s Sriniketh Chakravarthi: hiring a finished FDE “bust[s] the economics”; convert mid-management.  
- Tech Mahindra CEO Mohit Joshi: a significant proportion of Indian IT can be upskilled because they already have industry knowledge.  
- US compensation bands in the reprint are US figures. Do not treat them as India pay.

Index-only LinkedIn (not scraped; not ICP proof):  
- Shristi Achar, 5 days ago — reporter recap. Full post text unavailable.  
  https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shristi-achar-084b8b157_indias-largest-it-company-tata-consultancy-activity-7503117398158262272-7ROS  
  Excerpt: “India's largest IT company, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), wants to hire 8900 Forward Deployment Engineers this year, while Infosys targets up to 6000.” Reactions / comments / reposts / impressions unavailable.

**Direct quotes (ET via Apexon reprint):**  
- Navin Nair, VP digital engineering, data & AI, Mindsprint (Wipro): “What a forward-deployed engineer tries to do is collapse multiple roles into one or a few. A FDE needs to work directly with customers. So, they are business-aligned or domain-aligned, and they need to understand the business problem before building a solution.”  
- Sriniketh Chakravarthi, CEO, Apexon: “If I go to the market and say I want to hire an FDE, that’s a premium talent right now. That’s going to bust the economics. But I have a lot of people who can use our training machinery to turn raw, semi-finished material into FDEs.”  
- Mohit Joshi, CEO, Tech Mahindra: “I have no doubt that a lot of people who are there in the Indian IT services industry, a significant proportion of them would be upskilled and trained to become FDEs because many of them will already have detailed industry knowledge.”

**What appears to be working**  
The ratio (tens of thousands of seats vs ~1,500 hireable names) plus “collapse multiple roles” is a career object, not a vendor FDE explainer.

**Underlying idea**  
The next visible seat is client-embedded and outcome-owned. The mid-layer already has the domain. The file still describes them as delivery capacity. Conversion will happen to someone; the question is whether they are named as the person who owns the customer problem.

**CXO Hive opportunity / angle**  
Positioning. Do not recap Accenture’s 1,000 Google seats. Do not write “become an FDE.” Write the mid-year risk: if the firm is converting the middle, the brief that still says “I ran the machine” is the leftover Directors problem in a new costume.

**Suggested direction**  
Hook: “They want 8,900 FDEs. The market has about 1,500.” Then the collapse-the-roles sentence. Ask whether their brief still lists functions, or the customer problem they sat next to.

**Risks / saturation**  
Close to Friday’s Accenture-1,000-FDE post — keep the supply ratio and mid-layer conversion, drop Accenture/Google. Quess is staffing. Apexon is a vendor reprint of ET; say so. Reporter LinkedIn is not ICP. No qualified comments recovered. Do not invent India FDE salaries from the US bands.
